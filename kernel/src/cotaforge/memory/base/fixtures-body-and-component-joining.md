- Rigidity from lowest to highest: built-up, welded, cast. Order of clean profile given by the source: cast, welded, built-up.
- Built-up body: two dowels on the diagonal and as far apart as possible; dowel one size smaller than the assembly screw (the same in press work, because of shock and vibration); dowel engagement in the second part of 1.5 to 2 times its diameter; hardened dowels if there is shear or a tight tolerance is needed, unhardened if there is no shear.
- In several shops these dowels are called "registration pins": they locate two parts of the fixture or the machine relative to each other; they are not the pilot (locates the workpiece in the fixture) nor the guide (leads it in on entry). They are usually hardened and ground, often with an internal thread to extract them (DIN 7979 / ISO 8735).
- Engagement of the assembly screws: steel 1.5 D, cast iron 2 D, aluminum 2.5 D, fiber and plastic 3 D or more. Preferred thickness of the material relative to the screw: 2 times the outside diameter.
- Welded body: the fillet size is calculated with the thinnest section; after welding, annealing or normalizing may be needed.
- Cast body (H: beam height; t: thickness): single web t = √H (mm); double web t = 0.8 √H (mm); inside radii of 0.5 to 1.0 times t and outside radii of 0.18 to 0.2 times t. These are lower limits: consider the cooling rate to avoid hot spots.
