---
id: fixtures-clamping-toward-locators
type: practice
title: Clamp against the locators, with an equalizer if there are uneven surfaces or thin parts
sector: fixtures
applies_when: When choosing and placing clamps (screw, wedge, cam, toggle lever, strap)
not_when: A vacuum or magnetic clamp with a different design criterion
recognize: A clamp that presses at a point with no support underneath, or a lever that reaches dead center
typical_error: A toggle lever that goes past dead center and releases, or a clamp that flexes a thin wall
related:
- design-clamping-force-toward-supports
sources:
- "Applied Engineering, Jig and Fixture Design (summary of E. K. Hendriksen, Jig and Fixture Design Manual, 1973): clamps, equalizers and manual forces (read)."
- "The 0.2 coefficient in the tightening torque is the source's, \"for average shop conditions\": not checked with the shop."
---
- Equalizers: align the clamping force with the locators, distribute a concentrated force, clamp rough surfaces or surfaces at different heights and avoid deforming thin parts. Floating principle: the floating element is statically determinate and adjusts until the forces are balanced.
- Toggle lever: contact must be made shortly before dead center; at dead center it works only if everything is dimensionally exact; past dead center it slips and is useless.
- Self-locking wedge: typical taper between 1:20 and 1:15 (wedge angle up to 7°); with vibration, do not exceed 1:15.
- Clamping screw: T = 0.2 · D · P (torque, nominal diameter, clamping pressure), "representative of average shop conditions".
- Manual design forces: 30 lbf (≈ 133 N) to calculate the operating force and 90 lbf (≈ 400 N) as an occasional maximum for sizing; these are limit values, not operating values.
